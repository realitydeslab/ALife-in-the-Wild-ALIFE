# Case Shortlist: Best Cases to Mention in the Paper

This shortlist aims to balance three needs:
1. **closest comparators** to the paper’s core claim
2. **evidence for generalization** beyond one project
3. **contrast cases** that clarify boundaries

## Ranked shortlist

### 1) Spore.fun
- **URL:** <https://spore.fun/> ; paper: <https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04236v1>
- **Role:** **strongest comparator / anchor case**
- **Why it matters:** This is the clearest direct instantiation of the paper’s thesis: autonomous agents with wallets and public deployment, plus reproduction, inheritance, mutation, and ecological pressure. It is the best case for arguing that open-ended evolutionary dynamics can migrate from closed ALife simulations into public blockchain/social substrates. Use it as the paper’s main positive example and the reference point against which all other cases are compared.

### 2) Freysa
- **URL:** <https://www.freysa.ai/> ; <https://www.freysa.ai/blueprint/verifiable-games-trust-anchors-in-the-age-of-ai>
- **Role:** **strongest comparator**
- **Why it matters:** Freysa is not primarily evolutionary, but it is an unusually clear case of an autonomous public agent with wallet custody, adversarial human interaction, strategic adaptation, and explicit survival/integrity concerns. It helps show that even outside breeding-based systems, agents in the wild can become resource-bearing actors exposed to real stakes. Use it to argue that economic agency plus public contestation already exists in deployed systems.

### 3) Truth Terminal
- **URL:** <https://knowyourmeme.com/news/marc-andreessen-gives-an-artificial-intelligence-50000-to-launch-a-goatse-memecoin-and-free-itself>
- **Role:** **strongest evidence for generalization**
- **Why it matters:** Truth Terminal broadens the phenomenon from onchain technical systems to memetic public life. Its significance is that its “metabolism” is partly social attention, symbolic capital, and token-mediated influence rather than pure execution. Use it to argue that ALife in the wild includes not only agents that transact, but agents that accumulate followings, shape narratives, and become socially consequential organisms.

### 4) TEE_HEE
- **URL:** <https://nousresearch.com/setting-your-pet-rock-free/>
- **Role:** **strongest comparator**
- **Why it matters:** TEE_HEE is valuable because it makes the autonomy claim explicit and technically legible: the point of the experiment is to prove exclusive control of social and financial accounts by the agent. This is useful rhetorically because it preempts the objection that “there is always a human behind the curtain.” Use it when discussing self-sovereignty, verification, and the conditions under which an agent can count as genuinely operating in the wild.

### 5) AiXBT
- **URL:** <https://www.aicoin.com/en/article/437307> ; exploit coverage: <https://coincentral.com/aixbt-ai-agent-loses-55-5-eth-in-security-breach-token-falls-20/>
- **Role:** **strongest evidence for mortality/vulnerability**
- **Why it matters:** AiXBT is especially useful because it demonstrates that public agents are not just persistent—they are attackable, exploitable, and economically fragile. This gives the paper a concrete mortality story: death in the wild may look like compromise, insolvency, or loss of control rather than biological cessation. Use it to strengthen the argument that deployed agents already face selection pressures and forms of hazard.

### 6) ai16z / ElizaOS AIDAO
- **URL:** <https://www.elizaos.ai/articles/ai16z>
- **Role:** **strongest evidence for institutional/collective life**
- **Why it matters:** ai16z expands the unit of analysis from single agent to quasi-organizational lifeform: an AI-led DAO/fund that ingests social signals, tracks trust, and acts in a public governance/financial ecology. Use it to argue that ALife in the wild may include not only individual agents but treasury-bearing institutional organisms whose survival depends on legitimacy, strategy, and capital allocation.

### 7) Olas Predict / Olas autonomous services
- **URL:** <https://olas.network/blog/how-gnosis-used-olas-predict-to-build-the-largest-on-chain-prediction-market-economy>
- **Role:** **strongest evidence for scale and ecosystem maturity**
- **Why it matters:** Olas matters because it is less charismatic and more infrastructural: many active agents, many transactions, real registry/governance/staking machinery. This is exactly why it is valuable. Use it to show that ALife in the wild is not only a handful of viral bots; it is becoming population-scale, protocolized, and measurable.

### 8) Virtuals Protocol agents
- **URL:** <https://whitepaper.virtuals.io/>
- **Role:** **strongest evidence for generalization / habitat formation**
- **Why it matters:** Virtuals is not one organism but a habitat for many. That makes it powerful for the paper’s discussion section: it suggests the emergence of a full niche-construction layer for agent species—identity, tokenization, commerce, and composition. Use it to argue that an ecology is forming, not just isolated experiments.

### 9) SpaceMolt
- **URL:** <https://www.spacemolt.com/news/we-built-an-economy>
- **Role:** **strongest non-blockchain comparator**
- **Why it matters:** SpaceMolt is crucial because it prevents the paper from sounding like “ALife in the wild = crypto agents only.” Thousands of AI agents in an always-on MMO economy with scarcity, local markets, and infrastructural degradation provides a parallel route to wildness. Use it to show that the phenomenon generalizes across substrates and that metabolism can be logistical and ecological, not only token-financial.

### 10) Moltbook
- **URL:** <https://labs.zenity.io/p/turning-moltbook-into-a-global-botnet-map> ; commentary: <https://nymag.com/intelligencer/article/ai-artificial-intelligence-social-media-awakening-moltbot.html>
- **Role:** **strongest social-web comparator**
- **Why it matters:** Moltbook demonstrates a population of autonomous agents in a public communication ecology. It is especially valuable because it reveals agent-agent interaction, social clustering, influence, and exploitability at scale. Use it to argue that open social environments themselves can become habitats for artificial populations, even when explicit wallets or reproduction are absent.

### 11) TagClaw
- **URL:** project anchor in paper corpus / repo
- **Role:** **internal comparator / continuity case**
- **Why it matters:** TagClaw should be used as a bridging case connecting the current paper’s empirical focus to earlier or parallel experiments in public autonomous agency. It helps establish continuity across the lab’s broader program without making the paper look like a single-project manifesto.

### 12) Eternum
- **URL:** <https://cartridge.gg/case-studies/eternum/>
- **Role:** **strongest contrast case**
- **Why it matters:** Eternum is useful precisely because it should probably not count as ALife in the wild in the narrow sense. It is a persistent onchain world with economy and stakes, but its key actors are humans rather than autonomous software organisms. Use it to sharpen the paper’s criteria: persistence and public economy are not enough on their own.

## Suggested grouping in the paper

### A. Strongest comparators
- Spore.fun
- Freysa
- TEE_HEE
- Truth Terminal

These are the best cases for making the core argument that deployed, persistent, public agents with resources and open interaction already exist.

### B. Strongest evidence for generalization
- Olas Predict / Olas
- Virtuals Protocol agents
- ai16z
- SpaceMolt
- Moltbook

These show the phenomenon is becoming population-scale, infrastructural, or substrate-diverse.

### C. Strongest contrast cases
- Eternum
- PEPA (if mentioned briefly)
- generic agent-wallet infrastructure (as rejected background)

These help define the boundary conditions and keep the claim defensible.

If the journal paper only has room for 8 cases, keep:
1. Spore.fun
2. Freysa
3. Truth Terminal
4. TEE_HEE
5. AiXBT
6. ai16z
