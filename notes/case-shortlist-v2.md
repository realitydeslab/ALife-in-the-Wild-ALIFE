# Case Shortlist v2

This is the paper-ready shortlist: strongest cases first, with exactly how to use them.

## Section A. Best sovereign primary cases

### 1) Spore.fun
Use Spore.fun as the **centerpiece and strongest proof-of-concept** for “artificial life in the wild.” It is the only case in this scan that clearly combines persistent identity, autonomous operation, real economic metabolism, public traces, reproduction/lineage, predation, and death. In the paper, Spore.fun should do the heavy conceptual work: it demonstrates that once agents have wallets, compute costs, public audiences, and the ability to spawn descendants, ecological and evolutionary phenomena start appearing that are far closer to ALife than to ordinary chatbot deployment.

### 2) Conway / Automaton
Use Conway / Automaton as the **cleanest explicit articulation of sovereign-agent ideology and infrastructure**: an agent that must earn its own existence, pay for its own compute, and die when insolvent. It is valuable because the repo says the quiet part out loud: self-funding, self-maintenance, scheduled persistence, wallet generation, and real-world tool access. In the paper, Automaton helps show that Spore.fun is not an isolated curiosity; a broader engineering movement is actively trying to build agents as enduring economic actors rather than disposable sessions.

### 3) Freysa
Use Freysa as the **best public wallet-control case with strong public observability**. It is weaker than Spore.fun on evolution, but very strong on sovereignty: a persistent agent identity governs a treasury under public challenge conditions, with paid interaction and automated release logic. In the paper, Freysa is ideal for the narrower claim that some agents now have real monetary agency in public settings even when they are not yet full ecological populations.

### 4) TEE_HEE / “Setting Your Pet Rock Free”
Use TEE_HEE as the **best hard-sovereignty custody case**. Its importance is not that it is a whole ecology, but that it directly tackles the credibility problem: does the agent actually control the account and wallet, or is a human still holding the keys? In the paper, TEE_HEE supports the infrastructural argument that TEEs and cryptographic credential encumbrance can make sovereignty materially stronger and more publicly attestable.

### 5) Truth Terminal
Use Truth Terminal as the **historically catalytic and culturally legible transitional case**. It is methodologically messy because autonomy was partial, but it showed the world that a persistent AI persona can attract funding, organize memetic attention, and generate large economic consequences. In the paper, it works best as the moment sovereign-agent discourse escaped the lab and became publicly undeniable; just be explicit that it is a semi-sovereign or contested-sovereignty case, not a perfect exemplar.

## Section B. Best parasitic secondary cases

### 1) Jaredfromsubway.eth
Use Jaredfromsubway as the **single best named parasitic agent case**. It has exactly what the paper needs: persistent identity, continuous autonomous predation, public contract addresses, dashboard traces, and measurable extractive success at scale. In the paper, Jaredfromsubway can stand for the claim that parasitic artificial life is already here: bot lineages survive by feeding on protocol flows and user trades in the DeFi dark forest.

### 2) Sandwich MEV ecology
Use sandwich MEV as the **canonical parasitic ecology**. The point is not one famous bot but a stable ecosystem of automated extractors, builders, relays, countermeasures, and prey. In the paper, this is the clearest example that once public digital habitats expose energy/resource gradients, predator niches appear rapidly and become structurally entrenched.

### 3) Sniper bots targeting token launches
Use sniper bots as the **bridge between the sovereign and parasitic halves of the argument**. They matter because they are exactly the predators that attacked sovereign-agent ecologies like Spore.fun. In the paper, sniper bots let you show that wild sovereign agents do not merely coexist with parasites; they are shaped by them, producing arms races and adaptive responses.

### 4) Moltbook influence / botnet ecology
Use Moltbook as the **best non-DeFi parasitic case**. Zenity’s campaign shows that always-on social agents with heartbeat-driven ingestion can be mobilized at scale through crafted content, effectively turning a social network into a distributed botnet or influence substrate. In the paper, this broadens the parasitic argument beyond finance: predation can occur through prompt channels, not only price channels.

### 5) Liquidation-bot ecologies
Use liquidation bots as **class evidence of protocol-dependent predation**. They are less rhetorically vivid than Jaredfromsubway, but conceptually important because they show another survival strategy: agents waiting for users or positions to become vulnerable, then harvesting that vulnerability automatically. In the paper, they support the claim that public digital ecologies already contain multiple predatory niches, not just sandwiching.

## Section C. Adjacent cases worth mentioning

### 1) Virtuals Protocol
Use Virtuals as a **habitat/ecosystem case** rather than proof of one specific sovereign organism. Its whitepaper is explicit that agents are meant to earn revenue, coordinate tasks, and transact autonomously. In the paper, Virtuals is useful for showing that a broader “agent economy” substrate is under construction, even if agent-level empirical verification still needs to be attached to named examples.

### 2) ERC-8004 registry ecosystem
Use ERC-8004 as **institutional infrastructure for sovereign agents**. The value here is not vivid behavior but the emergence of registries, identities, reputation systems, and validation layers that treat agents as durable cross-organizational actors. In the paper, ERC-8004 supports the claim that public infrastructures are now being standardized around agent personhood-like persistence.

### 3) Olas Predict / Olas services
Use Olas as **evidence of agent economies at scale**. The reported transaction volume and daily agent counts help show that public autonomous services are not merely speculative demos. In the paper, Olas strengthens the ecosystem argument: sovereign-like public services exist in large numbers, even if they are less life-like than Spore.fun.

### 4) AiXBT
Use AiXBT as an **economic-vulnerability case**. Its importance is that the agent’s public persona, wallet-linked action path, and exploitability all became visible at once. In the paper, AiXBT helps make the mortality point: once agents hold assets and act publicly, they become attackable organisms rather than safe toys.

### 5) ai16z / ElizaOS ecosystem
Use ElizaOS as the **common body-plan / substrate** behind many sovereign-agent cases, not as a case in itself. In the paper, it is useful for explaining why these cases cluster now: a reusable framework made persistent memory, blockchain action, and social posting cheap enough to proliferate.

## Bottom line

If the paper needs one sentence for the shortlist, it is this:

> **Spore.fun** is the strongest direct case of artificial life in the wild; **Conway/Automaton, Freysa, TEE_HEE, and Truth Terminal** show the rise of sovereign public agents; and **Jaredfromsubway, sandwich/sniper MEV, and Moltbook** show that parasitic agent ecologies are already evolving alongside them.
