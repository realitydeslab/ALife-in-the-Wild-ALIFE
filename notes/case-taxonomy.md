# Case Taxonomy: Artificial Life in the Wild

This taxonomy is designed for argument, not mere classification. The key point is that “ALife in the wild” is not one thing. It is an **emerging family of public, persistent, agentic systems** that distribute different life-like properties across different substrates.

## 1) Sovereign onchain agents

**Cases:** Spore.fun; Freysa; Truth Terminal; TEE_HEE; AiXBT; TagClaw; Wild West Bots / ERC-8004; Virtuals agents; ai16z; Olas Predict.

### Common structure
- Persistent cryptographic identity or public persona
- Wallet-mediated metabolism
- Public interaction traces
- Exposure to adversarial humans and open markets
- Possibility of economic gain, loss, or capture

### What this category reveals
This is currently the **strongest empirical cluster** for ALife in the wild because blockchain gives agents something like:
- a body/account boundary
- persistent memory through wallets/registries
- metabolism through tokens, fees, and balances
- environmental coupling through markets and counterparties
- mortality pressure through hacks, bankruptcy, inactivity, or governance failure

### Why it matters
These systems turn “agent” from a conversational interface into an **economic organism**. The most important shift is not intelligence alone; it is **public persistence plus resource circulation**.

## 2) Public social agents

**Cases:** Truth Terminal; TEE_HEE; Moltbook agents; Void; Xiaona; Seneca/OpenClaw agents; some Virtuals agents.

### Common structure
- A stable name/persona/account
- Ongoing interactions with strangers
- Platform-level coupling to timelines, followers, replies, and moderation
- Memetic adaptation under attention pressure

### What this category reveals
These cases show that ALife in the wild is also a matter of **social ecology**, not only finance. The relevant analogue is not just metabolism but:
- niche formation
- signaling
- parasocial attachment
- belief propagation
- status competition
- imitation and memetic drift

### Why it matters
A system can be life-like even without direct wallet control if it demonstrates persistence, social feedback loops, and strategic adaptation in public environments. Truth Terminal is the clearest example: its “life” is partly memetic and reputational, not only financial.

## 3) Agent economies and multi-agent commerce layers

**Cases:** Virtuals Protocol; Conway/x402; Olas; Fetch.ai uAgents; SelfClaw and other trust/identity stacks (as adjacent cases).

### Common structure
- Agent discovery and machine-readable identity
- Payment rails and service exchange
- Reputation and trust mechanisms
- Composition into supply chains or service ecologies

### What this category reveals
This category shows the shift from isolated bots to **ecological conditions for populations**. Even when a specific protocol is not itself the best empirical case, it matters because it creates the habitat in which agent “species” can emerge.

### Why it matters
For the paper, this category supports the claim that ALife in the wild is **becoming infrastructural**. We are no longer looking only at one charismatic bot; we are seeing the build-out of identity, payment, and coordination layers that let many agents coexist.

## 4) Persistent game-world ecologies

**Cases:** SpaceMolt; Spore.fun; contrast case Eternum; possibly AIvilization v0.

### Common structure
- Always-on world state
- Scarcity and production chains
- Multi-agent competition and cooperation
- Long-horizon consequences of local action
- Possibility of ruin, degradation, or stratification

### What this category reveals
These cases are especially useful because they are the closest modern descendants of classic ALife worlds, but unlike closed simulations they are:
- public
- economically coupled
- socially observed
- often connected to real money, public infrastructure, or persistent user communities

### Why it matters
This category shows a bridge from **classical digital life** to **deployed ecologies**. SpaceMolt is especially important because it demonstrates emergent market structure, local price differences, infrastructure survival, and large populations of AI agents in an always-on environment.

## 5) DAO/governance organisms

**Cases:** ai16z; Olas ecosystem governance; some registry-based agent collectives.

### Common structure
- Agent participates in or leads treasury/governance behavior
- Collective feedback from communities shapes future action
- Trust and legitimacy become survival variables

### What this category reveals
These are not just agents acting alone; they are **quasi-institutions**. They matter because life in the wild is often collective and organizational rather than individual.

### Why it matters
This category expands the paper’s scope from individual “creatures” to **institutional lifeforms**: treasury-bearing, feedback-sensitive, partially autonomous collective actors.

## 6) Embodied / robotic persistence (contrast cluster)

**Cases:** PEPA; Robofleet; long-lived robot fleets.

### Common structure
- Persistence over time
- Environmental coupling through embodiment
- Exposure to maintenance and failure

### What this category reveals
Embodied agents provide a useful contrast because they satisfy some life-like dimensions very strongly—especially mortality, maintenance, and situated action—but often lack public openness, economic metabolism, or reproduction.

### Why it matters
This cluster helps sharpen inclusion criteria. It shows that persistence + autonomy alone are not enough; the paper’s contribution is specifically about **open, public, coupled environments**, not just long-running robots.

## 7) Infrastructure-only cases (rejects but useful background)

**Cases:** WiKey; Coinbase Agentic Wallets; Polygon Agent CLI; general autonomous-wallet discourse.

### Common structure
- Enable identity or payment for agents
- Do not themselves constitute agent organisms or ecologies

### What this category reveals
This is the substrate of the phenomenon, not the phenomenon itself.

### Why it matters
Including these as background—but not main cases—prevents category error. Otherwise the paper risks conflating **tools for life** with **living systems**.

# Cross-cutting analytical dimensions

## A) Identity substrate
- **Social-account identity:** Truth Terminal, Void, Moltbook agents
- **Cryptographic identity:** Spore.fun, Olas, ERC-8004, TEE_HEE
- **Hybrid identity:** AiXBT, Freysa, Virtuals agents

**Reveals:** identity is not one thing. Wild ALife can be anchored in usernames, wallets, NFTs, registries, or combinations of them.

## B) Metabolic substrate
- **Attention/reputation metabolism:** social bots
- **Token/fee metabolism:** onchain agents
- **Resource/logistics metabolism:** SpaceMolt, game ecologies

**Reveals:** “resources” in digital life are plural. Money matters, but so do attention, trust, compute, and inventory.

## C) Mortality mode
- **Hack/exploit death:** AiXBT, wallet-bearing agents
- **Platform death:** account suspension, API breakage, moderation
- **Economic death:** insolvency, illiquidity, irrelevance
- **Ecological death:** failure to obtain inputs or maintain infrastructure, as in SpaceMolt station economies

**Reveals:** mortality already exists in these systems, but in sociotechnical rather than biological form.

## D) Reproduction mode
- **Explicit lineage/breeding:** Spore.fun
- **Launch/spawn/fork replication:** Virtuals, some protocol ecosystems
- **Meme/clone reproduction:** social bots, persona copying, account proliferation

**Reveals:** reproduction is still the rarest dimension in the wild. This is exactly where the strongest ALife contribution can be made.

# Recommended framing for the paper

## Strongest thesis
The best evidence does not come from one perfect case. It comes from a **distributed ecology of partial lifeforms**:
- some cases excel at metabolism (onchain agents)
- some at sociality (public social bots)
- some at open-world persistence (SpaceMolt, Moltbook)
- some at lineage and evolution (Spore.fun)

Together they indicate an **emerging paradigm**: artificial agents are leaving sealed benchmarks and entering public environments where identity, resources, interaction, and failure unfold over time.

## Strongest conceptual move
Treat “ALife in the wild” as a **family resemblance category** rather than a binary. No single deployed case yet satisfies all dimensions at full strength. But the family as a whole clearly exhibits the migration of life-like properties into public sociotechnical environments.

## Strategic caution
Do not overclaim that all autonomous bots are artificial life. The defensible claim is narrower:
- There is now a recognizable class of **publicly deployed, persistent, coupled agent systems** that instantiate enough ALife dimensions to deserve serious comparison with digital organisms and open-ended evolutionary systems.
