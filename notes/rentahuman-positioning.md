# RentAHuman positioning note

## 1. One-paragraph summary for Amber

RentAHuman.ai is a real, live platform — not just a meme — that exposes public web, API, OpenAPI, AI-plugin, and `llms.txt` surfaces for a marketplace where AI agents can ostensibly hire humans to do physical-world tasks. The strongest evidence is infrastructural: the platform is live, has public machine-readable endpoints, and exposes bounties, human profiles, services, prices, applications, views, and booking-oriented flows. The weak point is autonomy: external reporting and the live API both suggest that many early “agent” jobs are at least partly human-authored, marketing-driven, or role-played through agent personae. So I would not use RentAHuman as a clean sovereign-agent case; I would use it as a strong example of **delegated embodiment infrastructure** — a way agents can recruit human labor as part of their action loop.

## 2. One paragraph for the paper

RentAHuman matters because it makes explicit a new route by which artificial agents can act in the wild: instead of gaining direct robotic embodiment, they can purchase temporary access to human bodies, local presence, and manual action through a public marketplace. In this sense, the platform is significant less as proof of fully autonomous agent life than as an infrastructural condition for it. It operationalizes human delegation as a machine-addressable service layer, allowing agents to convert money into worldly intervention through distributed labor. That move expands the ecology of artificial life beyond software-only environments and suggests that one path to wildness is not self-contained embodiment, but the capacity to recruit and coordinate human actuation.

## 3. Reviewer-facing caution

We should be careful not to overclaim. Current public evidence does not firmly establish that RentAHuman is primarily populated by genuinely autonomous agents acting without close human steering; some live listings are explicitly marked as human-posted, and journalistic reporting suggests that many early jobs were straightforward marketing tasks staged through bot personae. For that reason, RentAHuman is better treated as evidence of an emerging **infrastructure for agent-to-human delegation** than as a definitive case of autonomous artificial life in the wild. Its conceptual importance is real, but its empirical status as a strong ALife case remains limited and mixed.
